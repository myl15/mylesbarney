01 About

Researcher, Linguist,
Engineer

I'm a Computer Science PhD candidate at Brigham Young University, specializing in NLP, Machine Learning, and Machine Translation. My work lives at the intersection of deep learning and human language — with a particular focus on empowering people by improving accessibility through technology.

My primary research interests include low-resource neural machine translation and cross-lingual semantic transfer. I'm also a teaching assistant for CS270 and a member of the BYU Matrix Lab.

Beyond the lab, I'm driven by the conviction that language technology should preserve cultural heritage, empower marginalized communities, and build trust across linguistic divides. I like to envision a world where language barriers are no longer a part of our vocabulary.

02 Research & Projects

Current & Past Work

Active Research
2025 – Present

LADLE: LoRA Angle-Driven Language Ensemble

Investigates the use of angle-guided language selection for maximizing cross-lingual transfer in low-resource settings. LoRA adapters are trained on individual languages, angular distances between adapters are measured and used to guide language selection.

Cross-lingual Transfer NLLB-200 WikiMatrix SLURM / HPC MetricX-24
2025

Fine-tuning XTTSv2 on Low-Resource African Languages

Adapted XTTSv2 for 3 low-resource African languages (Xhosa, Igbo, Swahili), focusing on improving speech synthesis quality and reducing training data requirements. Integrated TTS models into full speech-to-speech translation pipelines.

PyTorch XTTSv2 African Languages Speech Translation
2024

Reimagining Question Answering as Machine Translation

Unified QA and MT under a single model using task-control tags, inspired by multilingual NMT tagging schemes. Evaluated zero-shot cross-lingual QA in Chinese–English and Chinese–Chinese settings using BERT and HuggingFace transformers.

BERT HuggingFace Zero-shot QA
2023

Improving Low-Resource MT Through Language Clustering

Developed a novel tagging approach for MNMT that clusters languages by position in the encoded latent space rather than surface-level family labels, aiming to maximize positive transfer. Explored multiple clustering and encoding strategies.

Scikit-learn MNMT Word Embeddings
03 Publications

Peer-Reviewed Work

2024

The Mysterious Case of Neuron 1512: Injectable Realignment Architectures Reveal Internal Characteristics of Meta's Llama 2 Model

Brenden Smith, Dallin Baker, Clayton Chase, Myles Barney, Kaden Parker, Makenna Allred, Peter Hu, Alex Evans, Nancy Fulda

Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) have an unrivaled and invaluable ability to "align" their output to a diverse range of human preferences. This work presents the Injectable Realignment Model (IRM) as a novel approach to language model interpretability and explainability. Inspired by earlier work on Neural Programming Interfaces, we construct and train a small network — the IRM — to induce emotion-based alignments within a 7B parameter LLM architecture. Analysis of the trained IRM's outputs reveals a curious pattern: across more than 24 training runs, patterns of IRM activations align themselves in striations associated with a neuron's index within each transformer layer, rather than being associated with the layers themselves. A single neuron index (1512) is strongly correlated with all tested alignments — highlighting a potential weak point in Meta's pretrained Llama 2 models.
